The term premium

Spot price divided by the same machine’s three-year committed price. Above 1.0, hourly buyers outbid customers who commit for three years — normal for scarce silicon. Below 1.0, the seller rents it today, with no commitment, for less than it charges people who signed a three-year deal.

Ninety days, us-west-2

The 32 GB card crossed under parity in mid-August and kept going. Over the same window, on the same four availability zones, the 96 GB card and the previous-generation L40S moved the other way. AWS is the only one of the three clouds that serves price history.

Same chip, different cloud

Today’s reading for every chip where both legs are published, three US regions per cloud. Dots are the median across SKUs and regions; the bar behind them is the range. The RTX PRO 6000 and the H100 both sit on opposite sides of parity depending on whose data centre they are in — so “this card is scarce” is never a fact about the card alone.

Method. AWS: ec2:describe-spot-price-history over the cheapest EC2 Instance Savings Plan, 3 year, All Upfront. Azure: Retail Prices API, spot meters over 3-year reservations, with the whole-term lump sum divided by 26,280 hours. GCP: Cloud Billing Catalog, Preemptible over Commit3Yr. The ratio is used rather than a price because both legs are the same SKU, so GPU count and machine size cancel and no hardware spec table is needed.

Limits. AWS spot has been an administered, smoothed price since November 2017, not an auction — this is a seller’s posted price, not an order book. GCP spot is administered outright, so its level carries little capacity information and only its changes do. A quoted price is not proof of available capacity: a low reading on a frontier part may be a phantom market rather than a soft one. The commitment leg is a list price that large buyers negotiate below, which biases every ratio upward — so a reading under 1.0 is the conservative case.
